The Ratatouille Fox kitchen guide

The techniques, pantry staples and planning habits that make weeknight dinners faster, tastier and far less stressful.

Technique

Cutting vegetables evenly

Even pieces cook at the same speed, so nothing ends up raw while the rest turns soft. For layered dishes, aim for slices about 3 mm thick; for stews, cubes of around 2 cm.

  • Rounds: courgettes, aubergines and tomatoes for layered bakes.
  • Strips: peppers and onions for skillets and stir-fries.
  • Dice: onions, carrots and celery for soups and sauces.

Keep fingertips tucked under in a “claw” shape and let the knife do the work.

Tools

The essential dinner toolkit

You don’t need a drawer full of gadgets. These few tools cover almost every recipe on this site:

  • A sharp chef’s knife and a stable chopping board.
  • A heavy frying pan or cast iron skillet.
  • A large lidded pot for soups and pasta.
  • A sturdy roasting tray and an ovenproof baking dish.
  • A digital thermometer for checking meat and fish.

Flavour

Cooking with herbs

Woody herbs such as rosemary, thyme and bay can handle long cooking, so add them early. Soft herbs such as basil, parsley, dill and chives lose their colour and fragrance with heat, so stir them in at the end.

Dried herbs are more concentrated: use about one teaspoon of dried herbs in place of one tablespoon of fresh.

Plan three, not seven

Plan three dinners a week and leave room for leftovers and spontaneous meals. It reduces waste and pressure.

Cook once, eat twice

Double soups, sauces and ratatouille. Freeze half in labelled portions for busy evenings.

Prep in batches

Wash and chop onions, peppers and herbs on the weekend to save time on weeknights.

Storage

Storing and freezing dinners

  • Cool cooked food quickly and refrigerate within two hours.
  • Use shallow containers so food chills evenly.
  • Label containers with the dish and date.
  • Most stews and soups freeze well for up to three months.
  • Thaw in the fridge overnight and reheat until piping hot throughout.
Ingredient spotlight

Olive oil in everyday cooking

Olive oil is the soul of Provençal cooking. Use a good extra virgin olive oil for drizzling, dressings and dipping bread, where its fruity, peppery flavour shines. For everyday sautéing and roasting, a standard olive oil works well.

Store bottles away from heat and light, and use them within a few months of opening for the best flavour.
